# Design Pillars

The rules below hold in every prototype. If a new mechanic breaks one of
these, it's the mechanic that's wrong, not the pillar.

## 1. Knowledge is the scarce resource

Meat spoils, fire dies, wood runs out — but the thing that actually decides
whether the tribe lives is **who knows how to do what**. Skill lives in
people. When ORR dies with fire-craft in his hands and no one watched him,
that knowledge is gone for good.

- **Head** = what you know (learned by watching / being taught).
- **Hand** = what you can actually do (built by trying / doing).
- A full head and an empty hand is a trap the game deliberately sets
  (see TEACH's "young try" prompt). Watching isn't doing.

## 2. Everything costs something

No free bonuses. A tool eats a whole action slot to make and real
materials to build. A hunt without a spear can bite back. Resting heals but
spends the day. Teaching spends the teacher's energy and the learner's
attention. The player is always spending.

## 3. Plans commit, then life walks

Across all three the player **decides up front, then watches it play out**
with limited or no mid-run control:

- TEACH: pick morning + afternoon for everyone, then hit RUN and watch.
- GREAT KILL: place your hunters, commit, then the kill runs on its own.
- THREE NIGHTS: pick weapon/ground at gates, then it's real-time but you
  can't re-plan mid-level.

The tension is in the *reading* (of terrain, of people, of the beast) and
the *commitment*, not in twitch correction.

## 4. Loss is permanent and it's the teacher

The old can die. A hunter can come home empty or not at all. Knowledge can
be lost. These aren't fail states to reload past — they're the memory the
game is built to create. "YOUNG SIT BY 🪏. NO WORD." lands because it's
earned.

## 5. The tribe is a system, not a hero

Nobody wins alone. GREAT KILL is explicit: five hunters, none of whom can
take the deer solo — BIRDDOG marks, LONGARM softens, SPEARWALL holds,
BREAKER ends, KEEPER steers. TEACH is the same idea slowed to a day: the
strong feed, the old teach, the young inherit. Even THREE NIGHTS, the solo
game, is framed as *the tribe sent you* — you're a limb, not the body.

## 6. One voice, small and true

The writing is compressed "cave tongue" — short, present-tense, physical.
Dinner speaks **one line** that names what the day *meant*, not what it
tallied. The stat row counts the meat; the voice counts the memory. Keep
this discipline everywhere: mechanics report numbers, fiction reports
meaning, and they never fight for the same slot.

## 7. Mobile-first, thumb-first

Every prototype is built for a phone held in one hand: big tap targets,
`svh` sizing, no reliance on hover, safe-area insets. New features get
designed for the thumb before the mouse.
